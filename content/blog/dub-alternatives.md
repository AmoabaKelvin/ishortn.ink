Dub built a well-designed, open-source link shortener. Its free plan still exists but is small: 25 new links and 1,000 tracked events a month, 30 days of analytics history and 3 custom domains for one user. Paid Links plans start with Pro at $30 a month ($27 a month billed yearly) for 1,000 new links, and Business is $90 a month ([Dub Links pricing](https://dub.co/pricing/links), prices checked 22 November 2026). Dub's main [pricing page](https://dub.co/pricing) now leads with its Partners plans, which start at $90 a month.

For a company running an affiliate or partner program, that may still be good value. Dub's recent [changelog](https://dub.co/changelog) is mostly partner and affiliate features, and that's clearly where the product is heading. If you're a freelancer, creator or small business who just wants branded short links, QR codes and click stats, 25 links a month runs out fast, and $30 a month is a lot to pay for the next step.

This guide covers the realistic alternatives, what each costs today, and how to move your links. We make iShortn, one of the options below, so we've kept the facts to what each company's own pricing page says (checked on 21 November 2026) and been clear about where each tool, including ours, is the wrong fit.

## What you probably used Dub for

Before comparing, it helps to be clear about which Dub features you actually need. Most people who are looking for an alternative used some mix of:

- **Branded short links** on their own domain (`go.yourbrand.com/offer`)
- **Click analytics**: countries, devices, referrers
- **QR codes** that point at a short link
- **An API** to create links from their app or automations
- **UTM tagging** for campaigns

Fewer people used Dub's newer features: conversion and revenue attribution, and partner/affiliate program management. If those are central for you, skip to "When to stay with Dub" at the end.

## 2. Short.io: the biggest free plan for custom domains

[Short.io](https://short.io/pricing) is the alternative most often recommended for people outgrowing Dub's free plan, and it's easy to see why. Its free plan includes 1,000 branded links in total (a lifetime limit, not monthly), 50,000 tracked clicks a month, and 5 custom domains. Paid plans are Hobby at $5 a month, Pro at $18, and Team at $48 (annual billing saves about 17%).

**Watch for:** the 1,000-link free limit is total, so a busy account will use it up eventually. Team members beyond one user start on the Team plan.

**Best for:** anyone whose top priority is a custom domain without paying. See [Short.io vs iShortn](/compare/short-io).

## 3. Rebrandly: branded links with an enterprise path

[Rebrandly](https://www.rebrandly.com/pricing) has been doing branded links for a long time. Its free plan is now small: 10 links a month and 100 tracked clicks a month, with watermarked QR codes and 1 custom domain, which can be a domain you already own or a free `.bio` domain from Rebrandly. Clicks above the tracking limit still redirect, but you won't see them in your stats. Paid plans start with Essentials at $11 a month billed yearly, which includes 2 custom domains; monthly billing costs more, so check the pricing page before you decide.

**Watch for:** the 100 tracked clicks on Free means you'll stop seeing data almost immediately on any real campaign.

**Best for:** larger brands that may eventually need its compliance and enterprise features. See [Rebrandly vs iShortn](/compare/rebrandly).

## 4. Bitly: the familiar name

[Bitly](https://bitly.com/pages/pricing) is the default many teams know. Free covers 5 links and 2 QR codes a month, with unlimited clicks and scans but no analytics history, and you can't change a link's or QR code's destination. Core is $10 a month (billed annually) for 100 links but no custom domain. A custom domain starts on Growth at $29 a month.

**Watch for:** the free plan isn't a realistic long-term home for anyone who outgrew Dub's free plan, and Growth is more than three times the price of the cheapest custom-domain plan elsewhere. We've written a longer guide to [Bitly alternatives](/blog/bitly-alternatives).

**Best for:** organisations that want a well-known vendor and already budget for it. See [Bitly vs iShortn](/compare/bitly).

## 5. Self-hosted Dub: keep Dub, skip the bill

Dub's core is open source under the AGPLv3 license, and its [README](https://github.com/dubinc/dub) says you can self-host it. If you have a developer and want to keep exactly the product you know, this is a real option.

**Watch for:** Dub depends on several separate hosted services, not just one server. You take on hosting costs, upgrades, uptime and security. Some features sit in an enterprise directory under a commercial license. For a small business without an engineer, this usually costs more in time than a paid plan.

**Best for:** technical teams that want full control of their data and are happy to run infrastructure.

## How to move your links off Dub

Moving is mostly about one question: **whose domain are your links on?**

### If your links use your own custom domain

You're in the best position. Your printed materials, bios and emails point to `go.yourbrand.com`, not to Dub. You can recreate the links elsewhere and then point the domain at the new service, and every existing link keeps working once the new service has the same short paths.

1. **Export from Dub.** Dub's help center explains [how to export links](https://dub.co/help/article/how-to-export-links): on the short links page, open the ⋮ menu and choose **Export links**. The default columns include the short link and the destination URL.
2. **Prepare the import file.** In iShortn, CSV import reads `url` (the destination), `alias` (the short path, e.g. `offer` for `go.yourbrand.com/offer`), and optionally `domain` and `note`. Rename Dub's destination column to `url`, put the part after the slash in `alias`, and set `domain` to your custom domain (rows without it are created on `ishortn.ink`).
3. **Add your domain to the new service first** (on iShortn, under custom domains on Pro), then import, so the links are created on that domain.
4. **Switch DNS** to the new provider's records. Expect a short window while DNS updates. Do it at a quiet time and test a handful of links afterwards.
5. **Keep your Dub analytics export** if you need historical numbers. Click history doesn't carry over between providers. Dub lets you [export analytics as CSV](https://dub.co/help/article/how-to-export-analytics).

Imported links count toward your monthly link allowance, so a large import may need a paid plan for the month you move.

### If your links use Dub's own short domain

Links on a domain Dub owns can't be moved, because only Dub controls where they redirect. Create new links elsewhere, update the places you control (bio, website, email signature, pinned posts), and keep the old ones live for as long as you can. Use this as the moment to set up a custom domain, so you never have to do this again. Our guide to [custom short links for your brand](/blog/custom-short-links-for-your-brand) walks through it.

## When to stay with Dub

To be fair to Dub: if you run a partner or affiliate program, need conversion and revenue attribution per link, or need up to 100 custom domains and three years of analytics retention, Dub's Business plan is built for that and the alternatives above (including iShortn) don't fully replace it. $90 a month can be cheap if links drive your revenue.

If your use is simpler (branded links, QR codes, honest click stats), you don't need to pay for a platform built for affiliate programs.
