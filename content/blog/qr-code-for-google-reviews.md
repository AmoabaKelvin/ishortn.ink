Most happy customers would leave you a review if it took ten seconds. The trouble is the ten seconds. They have to find your business on Google, scroll to the reviews, find the button and sign in. By then they've put the phone away.

A QR code takes them straight to the "write a review" box. One scan, pick the stars, type a sentence, done. This guide shows how to get your Google review link, turn it into a QR code, where to print it, and how to see which placement gets scanned.

## What you need before you start

- A Google Business Profile that you own or manage, and that Google has verified. Without one there's no review link to share.
- A computer. Google only generates its review QR code in a desktop browser, and the review link is easiest to copy there too.
- Ten minutes.

## Step 1: Get your Google review link

1. On a computer, go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and open your Business Profile.
2. Click **Read reviews**.
3. Click **Get more reviews**.
4. Click the **Copy** icon to copy the review link.

Paste that link into a new browser tab and check that it opens the review box for the right business. If you manage several locations, each one has its own link, so get each separately.

Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815) has the current wording of these buttons, which Google changes now and then.

## Step 2: Decide between Google's QR code and your own

The same **Get more reviews** window also shows a QR code. You can right-click it, save it and print it. For a lot of businesses that is enough, and it costs nothing.

Google's code is static, though. The review link is baked into the pattern, so you get two limits:

- **No scan count.** You'll see new reviews arrive, but you won't know how many people scanned and didn't finish, or whether the counter sign works better than the receipt.
- **No way to change where it points.** If you later want the code to open a page with Google *and* Tripadvisor, or your profile changes after a move, the printed code can't follow.

A **dynamic** QR code fixes both. It contains a short link, and the short link forwards to your review page. Each scan passes through the short link service, which counts it, and you can change the destination from your dashboard without reprinting. (Our [QR code guide](/blog/free-qr-code-generator-guide) covers static and dynamic codes in more detail.)

There's one catch with dynamic codes. They keep working only as long as the provider keeps the short link alive. Some generators switch your code off when a free trial ends, which is why people search "QR code expired". We wrote about that in [why QR codes stop working](/blog/qr-code-stopped-working). Before you print anything, check the generator's terms for what happens when you stop paying.

In our view, if the code goes on one sign by the till and you don't care about numbers, Google's code is fine. If it goes in more than one place, or you want to know whether it's working, use a dynamic code.

## Step 4: Print it where people have a free minute

A review takes a minute or two. Put the code where customers are waiting, not where they're rushing out the door.

**Places that tend to work:**

- **Counter or till sign**, while the card machine processes.
- **Table tents** in cafes and restaurants, read while waiting for the bill.
- **Receipts and invoices.** Service businesses (plumbers, cleaners, photographers) can add the code to the PDF invoice footer.
- **Thank-you cards** in delivery or e-commerce packages.
- **Waiting areas** at salons, clinics and garages.
- **The back of your business card**, handed over at the end of a job.

**Print checklist:**

- **Size.** A common rule of thumb is a scanning distance of about ten times the code's width. A code 2 to 3 cm wide is fine on a receipt or card held in the hand. A wall sign read from a metre or two away needs a code of 10 cm or more.
- **Quiet zone.** Leave a blank margin around the code. Text or borders that touch the edge make it harder to scan.
- **A line of text above it.** "Enjoyed your visit? Scan to leave us a Google review" works better than a bare square. People scan more when they know what they'll get.
- **Test the printed copy**, not the file on your screen. Scan it with an iPhone and an Android phone, in the light where it will hang.

## Step 5: Ask, and stay inside Google's rules

The code does half the work. The other half is someone saying, "If you were happy with this, a Google review helps us a lot. The code's right there." A personal ask at the right moment does more than any sign. Ask once, kindly, and leave it there. Google's policy doesn't allow pressuring customers to review while they're on the premises.

Google is strict about how you ask:

- **No incentives.** Discounts, freebies or prize draws in exchange for reviews count as fake engagement under Google's policy. Reviews collected that way can be removed and your profile can be penalised. Don't print "10% off for a review" next to the code.
- **Don't filter for happy customers.** Google's policy also bans selectively asking for positive reviews only. "Scan to tell us how we did" is fine. A page that sends 5-star ratings to Google and everyone else to a private form is "review gating" and breaks the rules.
- **Customers need a Google Account** to post a review. Most Android users and many iPhone users are already signed in. Some will bounce at the sign-in step, which is one reason scans will always outnumber reviews.

## Step 6: Track scans and compare them with new reviews

After a week or two, open the code in your dashboard. For each code you'll see total scans, scans over time, country and city, and device. Read the numbers like this:

- **Scans by placement.** Since each placement has its own code, compare them. If the receipt code gets 40 scans and the counter sign gets 3, move the sign or change its wording.
- **Scans vs new reviews.** Your Business Profile shows how many reviews came in. If 50 people scanned and 6 reviewed, the code is doing its job and the drop-off happens on Google's side (sign-in, writing). A short ask in person usually helps more than a new design.
- **Scans over time.** A spike after you started asking out loud tells you something. So does a code that has gone quiet because the sign fell behind the coffee machine.

Scan counts are aggregate. You'll see "12 scans on Android from Kumasi", not who scanned. That's fine for this job.

## Change the destination later without reprinting

This is where a dynamic code earns its keep. Some situations we see:

- **You move or rebrand** and your Business Profile changes. Paste the new review link into the existing code.
- **You want reviews on more than one site.** Point the code at a simple page that lists Google, Tripadvisor and Facebook, and let customers choose. Send everyone to the same page, happy or not, to stay within Google's rules.
- **The season changes.** In December the counter code could point at your gift card page for a few weeks, then go back to reviews. The printed sign never changes.
